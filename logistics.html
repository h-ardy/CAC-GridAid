What is FEMA?

FEMA, the Federal Emergency Management Agency, is the part of the U.S. Department of Homeland Security that helps communities prepare for, respond to and recover from disasters.

Its Hazard Mitigation Grant Program pays for projects that reduce damage from future disasters, including permanent backup generators at critical buildings. The money becomes available after the President declares a major disaster. Counties apply through their state, and FEMA can cover up to 75% of a project. The county and its local partners pay the rest, in cash or in donated work and materials.

That is why this page splits every cost into what FEMA could pay and what the county pays. A grant isn't guaranteed: the state chooses which projects to fund, and the county needs a FEMA-approved hazard mitigation plan to apply.
